# M5 — Economy depth

## Delivered
- All production chains through mission 6 are data (recipes, buildings, resources under footprints).
- Money: the treasury pays wages (policy, default 5/day per worker); households keep savings and buy food and goods at markets at the current price; taxes (wage tax by class plus 5%/day of savings above 30) are collected from houses covered by a forum. A ledger records every flow by category (taxes, sales, exports, grants / wages, construction, imports, upkeep, festivals, military, other) per day and per year.
- Prices: each good's market price follows scarcity (days of stock vs yesterday's consumption), 60–200% of base.
- Annona (grain dole) policy: staples free for poor households (default) or for everyone.
- Household goods (pottery, clothing, oil, wine, furniture, glassware, dyed clothing, jewelry) are bought at markets and wear out over time.
- Food: each unit feeds `2 x nutrition` meals; variety comes from distinct food types in the larder.
- Trade (`data/defs/trade_partners.json`): partners with land or sea routes, opening cost, visit interval, goods bought/sold with yearly appetite. Prices move with how much of that appetite has been used. Land caravans (three traders) walk in from the map edge to the nearest trade post, exchange, and leave; sea routes dock a ship at a harbor. Players set per-good import/export mode and level; porters stage exports at trade posts.
- UI: finance window (ledger + wage, taxes, annona), trade window (routes, prices, per-good settings), goods window with prices and trade volumes.

## Exit check
`tests/scenarios/economy_3000.json` runs 10 game years (192k ticks, about 3 s headless) without collapse: population stays above 1,200, the treasury stays above -40,000, no allocation inside `tick()`. The headless `--verbose` dump explains every flow (money by category, goods made/used/imported/exported/price, household savings and hunger, happiness parts).

## Notes
Fine balancing (wage/price/tax levels, building throughput) is deferred to the campaign missions, where cities are laid out deliberately rather than generated.
